# Seeing health through the streets: neighborhood environments and self-rated health among older adults in Brazil

Introduction: Self-rated health is a robust predictor of morbidity, mortality, and health care use, particularly in older populations. As people age, their health becomes increasingly shaped by the immediate environments in which they live. However, evidence on how neighborhood physical conditions influence perceived health in low- and middle-income settings remains limited. We investigated the association between neighborhood physical characteristics and poor self-rated health among older adults in Brazil. Methods: We used data from the ELSI-Urbe study, which includes 2,766 participants from the second wave (2019–2021) of the ELSI-Brasil cohort nested in 218 neighborhoods, whose characteristics were assessed through a standardized virtual audit using Google Street View across 20 major Brazilian municipalities in 2024. Six indicators, grouped into three domains (urban infrastructure: pathway accessibility, and surface and continuity; traffic: markings and controls, and speed and bus lane; physical disorder: absence of infrastructure and vegetation, and absence of buildings and green spaces), captured features of the built environment. The outcome was self-rated health, dichotomized as poor versus good. Associations were estimated using Poisson regression with robust variance, accounting for clustering at the census tract level and adjusting for sociodemographic characteristics. Results are presented as prevalence ratios (PR) with 95% confidence intervals. Results: Participants had a mean age of 66 years (SD = 10), and 53% were women. Favorable neighborhood features were consistently associated with better perceived health. Greater pathway accessibility (PR: 0.88; 95% CI: 0.83–0.93) and the presence of traffic markings and controls (PR: 0.86; 95% CI: 0.79–0.94) were associated with a lower prevalence of poor self-rated health. In contrast, physical disorder, captured by the absence of infrastructure and vegetation, was associated with a higher prevalence of poor self-rated health (PR: 1.08; 95% CI: 1.01–1.15). Conclusions: Neighborhood physical environments are closely linked to older adults' perceptions of their health in Brazilian cities. These findings underscore the importance of age-friendly urban design and highlight the value of scalable, low-cost virtual audit methods for capturing environmental determinants of health. Improving local infrastructure and reducing physical disorder may support healthier aging and enable older adults to age in place with dignity.
